Find the layer where the problem actually lives.

Three different companies, three different decades of technology, one repeated method. This is the part that transfers.

The method

Five steps, run in order

01

Frame the problem

The presented problem and the real problem are usually different, and the gap between them is where most of the value is. Too many meetings is a symptom. Fourteen incompatible KPI definitions is a symptom. The question is always which layer the failure actually sits at.

Evidence · AMC diagnosis · Kaseya diagnosis
02

Build the system, not the fix

A point solution holds until the next thing arrives without a defined way to absorb it. The test of an operating model is not whether it solves today's problem, it is whether the next partner, the next acquisition or the next workstream plugs into something that already exists.

Evidence · 16 business units · 14 acquisitions
03

Establish the cadence early

Reporting and cadence look like administrative layers you add once execution problems become visible. They are not. They are the operating infrastructure, and installing them early is what makes risk visible while leadership still has cheap options.

Evidence · AMC, what I'd change now
04

Make inspection cheap enough that it always happens

Quality control that depends on someone remembering to be careful is not quality control. Define what good looks like, inspect against it automatically, and separate the thing that produces work from the thing that judges it.

Evidence · a governed quality layer
05

Treat every failure as a defect

A failure that gets fixed once will happen again. A failure that gets diagnosed, encoded as a check and propagated back to the source changes the system's behaviour permanently. This is the difference between being careful and being engineered.

Evidence · the rule-capture loop
What it produces

Three principles I did not start with

Each of these came out of getting something wrong first.

Integrate the layer, not the company.

Roll-ups get framed as a speed question. The better question is which layer to unify. Names and products stay; systems, metrics, reporting and planning become common. Get that right and the portfolio absorbs almost anything.

Cadence is infrastructure, not administration.

Every month you wait to install reporting and rhythm, the intervention gets more expensive and problems get further along before anyone sees them.

A system that evaluates its own reasoning is exposed to the assumptions that produced the error.

This is why generation and evaluation are separated, and why more review inside one agent produces more confident wrong answers rather than fewer.
