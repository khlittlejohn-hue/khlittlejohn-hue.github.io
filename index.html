The method

Decision infrastructure

I have entered five industries cold and spent most of my career outside the organization I was trying to move. The method is what is left when you cannot rely on knowing the domain or directing anyone.

Five moves. They depend on one another rather than happening in order, and the last one is the only test that counts.

  1. Find the layer

    Integrate the layer, not the company.

    The presented problem and the real problem are usually different. Fourteen incompatible KPI definitions is a symptom; a capital allocation problem is the cause.

  2. One basis for comparison

    The decision requires a common basis for comparison.

    Not more visibility, which is a different thing and does not produce it. You cannot allocate capital across a portfolio you cannot compare.

  3. Establish cadence

    Cadence earns its authority.

    Reporting and rhythm are operating infrastructure, not administration. Installed early, they make risk visible while leadership still has cheap options.

  4. Nothing grades its own work

    Inspection should be cheap.

    A system that evaluates its own reasoning is exposed to the assumptions that produced the error. The principle is longstanding; the automated mechanism is new, and the AI work is what forced it.

  5. Reach the decision

    The real test is whether leadership does something different because of the infrastructure.

    Not whether it produces reports. At Kaseya the clearest proof was an acquisition the executive team declined on analysis I built. I did not make that call, and it was not mine to make.

Around all five

Every failure becomes a permanent check.

A correction that lives in a document gets forgotten. One that lives in a gate does not. Applied to machine-produced work, the same rule reads: fabrication is a system bug.

Point of view

What I have not resolved

Three questions the method raises and does not answer.

Cadence should be installed early, but early is exactly when you have no credibility to install it.

Solving one visible thing first and spending the credit immediately has worked for me. That is a sequencing trick rather than an answer.

What is the honest price of the status quo?

Every proposed change is measured against its cost. Almost nothing is measured against the cost of continuing as-is, because that cost appears on no line item.

When does adding another quality gate stop paying for itself?

Gates cost time on every run forever, to prevent an error that happened once. I suspect some of mine are no longer worth what they cost.
