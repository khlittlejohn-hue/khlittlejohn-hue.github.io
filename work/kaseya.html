Case Study 01 · Kaseya · 2018 to 2020Acquisition integration and executive reporting

One operating model across 10 acquired companies.

Kaseya grew by acquisition, and each acquired company arrived on its own systems and its own KPI definitions. I built the KPI and executive reporting and the post-close integration rhythm that put 10 acquired companies on one shared set of plans, KPIs and definitions.

01Challenge

10 companies, 10 sets of definitions

Kaseya was executing an aggressive acquisition strategy under private equity ownership, buying companies and folding them into a consolidating IT management portfolio. The company had serious discussions with 14 targets and completed 10 acquisitions.

Each acquired company arrived on its own systems and its own KPI definitions. A KPI in one company did not mean what the same KPI meant in another, so the executive team could not compare the businesses it now owned on a common basis. After each deal closed, the integration and the reporting came to my team.

02What I owned and did

Reporting, executive forums and integration

I was a trusted advisor to the CEO and worked directly with the C-suite through the scale-up. The work fell into 3 groups.

Shared measures and financial reporting

  • I built the KPI definitions and the executive reporting the executive team ran the portfolio on.
  • I reported the financial health of each acquired portfolio to the C-suite through P&L roll-ups, revenue forecasts and budget-versus-actual reports.
  • That reporting informed the executive team’s investment and acquisition decisions.

Executive forums and reporting

  • I led the weekly leadership meetings, and ran and prepared the executive town halls.
  • I created the board materials and sat in the quarterly board meetings.
  • I joined calls with the sponsor as the CFO’s backup on portfolio questions.
  • I contributed to the annual and multi-year strategic planning process.

Integration processes and systems

  • I built the post-close integration rhythm that brought each acquired company onto the shared plans, KPIs and definitions while it kept its external brand.
  • I integrated each acquired company’s HR, FP&A and CRM systems onto Kaseya’s unified platform (Bamboo, Adaptive Planning and Salesforce), working with each company’s C-suite, and trained their staff on the new platforms.
  • I worked with the Kaseya CTO on technology headcount across the acquired portfolios.
03Decision

What to make common, and what to leave alone

A roll-up is usually discussed as a question of speed. Integrate fast and you risk damaging what you bought. Leave each company alone and the executive team owns a set of businesses it cannot compare. I did not think speed was the real decision. The real question was what to make common.

The acquired companies kept their names and their products, which were what their customers valued. The integration work went into what sat underneath: the systems, the KPI definitions, the reporting and the budget-versus-actual view. Each integration usually took about 6 months, and several ran at the same time.

The tradeoff was that the companies never looked identical from the outside. The executive team did not need them to. It needed one consistent way to read a portfolio that kept changing shape.

04Results

What changed, and how it is measured

  • 10 acquired companies integrated over about 2.5 years, from 2018 to 2020.
  • The 10 acquired companies worked from one shared set of plans, KPIs and definitions, and I trained their staff on the new platforms.
  • When further acquisitions were under consideration, leadership came to my team for a go or no-go read on current portfolio performance.
  • I contributed the financial narrative and data room materials behind the $500M+ growth financing round from TPG and Insight Partners in May 2019.

The acquired companies worked from the shared definitions, and the reporting informed the executive team’s investment and acquisition decisions.

05What carries forward

Decide early what has to be common

In a growing portfolio, the first decision is which parts must be shared and which can stay local. At Kaseya the shared part was the systems, the KPI definitions and the reporting. Once those were in place, each new acquisition joined a model that already existed.
